What Your Portfolio Is

A folder on your own computer that you build one file at a time, all semester.

Almost everything you make in this course ends up as a PDF. Your portfolio is simply the folder where you keep all of them, in order, from week 1 to the end of the term. You create it, you name it, you back it up, and you hand the whole thing in at the end.

It is not stored in Canvas as you go, and it is not stored in these course pages. These pages don't remember anything — every time you close one, whatever you typed into it is gone. That's why each activity ends with a Save as PDF button: that PDF is the thing that lasts, and your portfolio folder is where it goes.

Step one: make the folder, today

Create a folder called CSC141-portfolio. Put it wherever you'll actually be able to find it in December:

  • Your college Google Drive — you already have it with your @student.mendocino.edu login, and it's reachable from any computer.
  • A thumb drive — fine, as long as it's one you don't lose.
  • Your computer's hard drive — fine too, as long as you're confident the machine will survive the semester.

Then, every time you finish something, move it in right away — either the original PDF or a copy of it. Not at the end of the module, not at the end of the term: as you finish it. That single habit is the whole trick, and it's what guarantees the folder is complete in week 17 without a scramble.

Caution

The Downloads folder is where portfolios go to die. The most common way this goes wrong is a student who does the work, exports the PDF, leaves it wherever the browser dropped it, and can't find it three months later. Your browser saves to Downloads by default — rename the file and move it into CSC141-portfolio in the same sitting, while you still remember what it was.

The shape of it

One folder. Flat — no subfolders. Every file a PDF. Every filename starting with a two-digit number:

  • CSC141-portfolio/
  • 01-ten-things-baseline.pdfweek 1 — your starting list
  • 02-m1-bias-analysis.pdfweek 3 — your first graded artifact
  • 03-…and so on, all semester

That's the entire system. The number keeps the folder in order, so that at the end of the term the story of your semester reads top to bottom.

Graded Items and Ungraded Items — Both Belong

Some files are big assignments with their own grade. Some are short pieces that are simply required. All of them are part of your portfolio.

Graded

The bigger pieces — the module artifacts. Each one is a real assignment with its own rubric, submitted to Canvas and scored on its own.

Your bias analysis, your prompt casebook, your policy brief, and the rest. These are the substantial work of the course.

Each also goes into your portfolio folder, because at the end you hand in the whole collection.

Ungraded

The smaller pieces — things like your week-1 "10 Things" list. No rubric, no score, nothing to get wrong.

They're quick, and some are deliberately ungraded so you'll answer honestly instead of writing what you think earns points.

They are still required. A portfolio missing them is incomplete.

Note

The part students usually miss: "ungraded" means that file has no score of its own. It does not mean it doesn't count. Your portfolio is graded as a collection at the end of the term, and a collection with holes in it is graded as a collection with holes in it. The 10 Things list is worth no points by itself and is one of the most important things in the folder — it's the "before" picture that makes your growth visible in week 17.

Naming Your Files

Two-digit number, hyphen, short description, .pdf. That's it.

NN-short-description.pdf

  • NN — a two-digit number that belongs to that specific artifact. The 10 Things list is always 01. The Module 1 bias analysis is always 02. Each assignment tells you its number when it's assigned. Use the leading zero — 01, not 1 — or your computer will sort 10 before 2 and the order falls apart.
  • short-description — a few words, lowercase, hyphens instead of spaces. Enough that you know what it is without opening it.
  • .pdf — everything in the portfolio is a PDF. Every course page that produces an artifact has a Save as PDF button that makes one for you.

The number is fixed, not a running count

This is the part worth reading twice. The number is attached to the artifact, not to the order you happened to file things. It is the same number for everyone in the class. You don't choose it and you don't increment it yourself — you're told what it is.

Which means a missing number is a missing artifact, and that's deliberate. If you never do the Module 1 bias analysis, there is no 02 in your folder. Not a placeholder, not a renumbered 02 holding something else — just nothing:

  • CSC141-portfolio/
  • 01-ten-things-baseline.pdf
  • (no 02 — this artifact was never completed)
  • 03-…a later artifact, under its own number

That gap is the point. It's how you can tell at a glance what's missing, and it's how your portfolio gets read at the end of the term. Sliding the next file up into the empty slot doesn't fill the hole — it just hides it, and it puts every later file under the wrong number.

Caution

Never renumber to close a gap. If you complete something late, it goes in under its own number, wherever that lands — the folder re-sorts itself the moment you do. Renumbering to make the sequence look continuous is the one thing that genuinely breaks this system.

One PDF can hold more than one thing

Some course pages bundle several pieces into a single export. The week-3 studio is the first: its Save as PDF produces one file containing your bias analysis, your privacy-policy outline, and your Module 1 reflection together.

That's still one file, so it gets one number — 02. The privacy outline and the reflection don't get numbers of their own; they're inside 02. If an assignment ever produces two separate PDFs, you'll be told both numbers.

What Goes In It

Two files so far. The rest arrive as each module closes.

Filed already

Two artifacts so far. Their numbers, exact filenames, and whether each is graded are all on the Portfolio Checklist page in Canvas — that's the page that stays current as the course goes, so it isn't repeated here.

Still to come

You'll add roughly one file per module, plus the final assembly. Each one has a number waiting for it, and you'll be given that number when the assignment is. Here's the shape of the rest of the semester — the details come with each module, so there's nothing to do about these yet:

  • Module 2A prompt engineering casebook — the same task run through Gemini plus two tools you pick, compared. Graded
  • Module 3An AI-assisted research exercise with full disclosure of what you used, and the first version of your Personal AI Code. Graded
  • Module 4A bias audit of a real AI output, and a personal data-protection and security assessment. Graded
  • Module 5A mini-project applying AI to your own field of study or work. Graded
  • Module 6An AI policy recommendation brief, and version 2 of your Personal AI Code — revised, with your reasoning for every change you made since version 1. Graded
  • End of termA revisit of your "10 Things" list — revised, plus a new work-focused list — and a synthesis reflection tracing the whole arc. This is where your 01 file finally earns its keep. Graded
Note

A module reflection closes each module — a short piece of writing about what changed in your thinking. Sometimes it's bundled into that module's artifact PDF, as it is in Module 1; sometimes it'll be its own file. Either way it belongs in the folder, and the reflections are what the final portfolio grade actually reads most closely.

How the Portfolio Is Graded

Two separate things are happening, and it helps to keep them apart.

Each artifact, on its own

When you finish a module artifact, you submit it to Canvas and it's graded against that assignment's rubric, like any other assignment.

That score lands in the Module artifacts category, which is 40% of your course grade.

The portfolio, as a collection

At the end of the term you hand in the whole folder, together with a synthesis reflection tying it together.

That's the Semester portfolio category — 20% of your course grade — covering your module reflections and the final assembly.

So a graded artifact is scored twice, in two different ways: once for the work itself, and once as part of a collection that's judged on completeness and on how well the pieces connect. An ungraded item is only in the second of those — which is precisely why leaving it out costs you something even though it was never worth points on its own.

Verify

Canvas is the system of record for every date and score. This page describes what the portfolio is and how to keep it; it deliberately doesn't list due dates, because a date printed here would eventually disagree with Canvas and you'd have no way to know which one was wrong. Check Canvas for when things are due, and for what you've been given credit for.
