Step 2: Gemini Is Your Baseline — Everything Else Is Your Call

How tool choice works for the rest of the semester.

Here's the deal. Gemini is the one tool the course can assume everyone has, so it's the baseline: demonstrations use it, screenshots match it, and any activity that doesn't say otherwise assumes it. That common starting point is what makes step-by-step instructions actually work for a class where nobody needs prior experience.

Past that baseline, the choice is yours. Once you're comfortable, use whatever tool you like — unless an activity specifically directs you to one, in which case it will say so and tell you why. Several assignments ask you to run the same task through more than one tool and compare; those keep Gemini as the shared starting point so the class has something in common to talk about, and let you pick the rest. You will never need an account outside the college to finish this course.

Nothing in this course requires you to pay for anything. Claude and Microsoft Copilot show up as alternates where comparing them teaches something.

Step 3: What to Expect When You Talk to AI

Three things to keep in mind before you start.

  1. AI tools are conversation partners, not search engines. You talk to them in natural language. The more specific and clear your request (called a "prompt"), the better the response.
  2. Different tools give different answers. Two AI systems asked the same question may produce very different results — in quality, tone, accuracy, and length. That's not a bug; it's a feature of how these systems work, and you'll learn why later in this module.
  3. AI can be wrong. Every AI system makes mistakes — sometimes small, sometimes significant. Part of being AI-literate is learning to check and verify what the tool gives you. We'll cover that skill throughout this course.

Your First Practice: Compare Three Tools

About 15 minutes Activity M1L1-A1 · ungraded

Now let's put the tools side by side.

  1. Open Gemini plus two other AI tools of your choice from the lists above, in separate browser tabs.
  2. In each tool, type this exact prompt: 
    List three ways AI is used in everyday life that most people don't realize.
  3. Read the three responses. Notice differences:
    • Did they give the same examples?
    • Which response was easiest to understand?
    • Did any response include something surprising or unexpected?
  4. Jot down 2–3 brief notes about what you noticed. These are for your own reflection — no submission required for this activity.

This is an ungraded practice activity designed to get you comfortable with the tools and start noticing how AI systems differ. You'll build on this comparison skill throughout the course.
