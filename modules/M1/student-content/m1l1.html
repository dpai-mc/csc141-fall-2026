Welcome — Let's Get You Set Up with AI Tools

Hi! Before we dive into how AI works, you need access to a few tools. This lesson walks you through getting set up with Gemini (your primary tool for this course, accessed through your college Google account) and a couple of other free AI tools so you can start comparing how different systems respond.

Step 3: What to Expect When You Talk to AI

Before you start generating prompts, here are three things to keep in mind:

  1. AI tools are conversation partners, not search engines. You talk to them in natural language. The more specific and clear your request (called a "prompt"), the better the response.
  2. Different tools give different answers. Two AI systems asked the same question may produce very different results — in quality, tone, accuracy, and length. That's not a bug; it's a feature of how these systems work, and you'll learn why later in this module.
  3. AI can be wrong. Every AI system makes mistakes — sometimes small, sometimes significant. Part of being AI-literate is learning to check and verify what the tool gives you. We'll cover that skill throughout this course.

Your First Practice: Compare Three Tools (Activity M1L1-A1)

Now let's put the tools side by side. Follow these steps:

Step-by-step instructions:

  1. Open Gemini in one browser tab and two other AI tools of your choice in separate tabs.
  2. In each tool, type this exact prompt: List three ways AI is used in everyday life that most people don't realize.
  3. Read the three responses. Notice differences:
    • Did they give the same examples?
    • Which response was easiest to understand?
    • Did any response include something surprising or unexpected?
  4. Jot down 2–3 brief notes about what you noticed. These are for your own reflection — no submission required for this activity.

This is an ungraded practice activity designed to get you comfortable with the tools and start noticing how AI systems differ. You'll build on this comparison skill throughout the course.
