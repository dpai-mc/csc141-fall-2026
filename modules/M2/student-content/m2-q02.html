What You’ll Do

One task, run through three tools with the same prompt, written down and read.

Read this section

Your casebook is one task, run through three tools with the same prompt, written down and read. You have the whole studio block to build it, and it is the artifact Module 2 is graded on.

Everything you need to judge the outputs - what a casebook is, how to pick a task, what the five criteria mean, and what a synthesis is for - is in this week’s lesson, Building Your Prompt Engineering Casebook. This page is where you do the work.

Part 1 - the task and the prompt

Name the task in a sentence, then write the prompt you will use. Write it deliberately, using the anatomy from Anatomy of a Good Prompt: role, task, context, constraints, format. You need at least three of the five. This is the prompt you will send to all three tools, unchanged.

Part 2 - three entries

One for each tool. Each entry holds:

  • which tool you used;
  • the prompt - the same one every time, so you write it once in Part 1 and it appears with every entry in your PDF;
  • the output you got back, in full;
  • your critique - at least three of the five criteria, two to four sentences, citing the specific thing in the output that led to it.

Part 3 - the synthesis

Two hundred to three hundred and fifty words, written after you have read all three. At minimum: one pattern you saw across the outputs, what it tells you about your prompt or your task, and one change you would make to the prompt now that you have seen three attempts at answering it.

Then - your Module 2 reflection

Written at the studio close, in the last block. It goes in the same PDF.

Format & length

  • Typed into this page, or pasted in from wherever you drafted it.
  • Part 1: the task in one sentence; the prompt in full.
  • Part 2: three entries, each with tool name, full output, and a critique of two to four sentences naming at least three criteria.
  • Part 3: 200–350 words.
  • Module reflection: 3–5 sentences answering all three prompts.
  • Paste outputs in full. If one is very long, paste the whole thing anyway - an output trimmed to its good parts is not a record of what happened.

Tools

Gemini plus any two of your choice. Three results in total.

Read this section

Gemini (your college Google account) plus any two other tools of your choice. Three results in total.

Gemini is the shared one so that everyone’s casebook has a column in common - which is what makes the discussion next Monday work. The other two are yours to pick.

All three must be tools you can open in a browser and actually send your prompt to. Free tiers are fine. Copilot is available through your college Microsoft account if you would like a second college-provided tool, and it is the fallback if Gemini gives you trouble. You do not need to open an account with any outside company to do this assignment - if your two picks would require one and you would rather not, use Copilot and one other.

Caution

A model you only read about doesn’t count as one of the three. This week’s other lesson has you research an openly-distributed model without running it. That research is a different exercise. The casebook needs three outputs you actually received.

How It’s Graded

100 points, four dimensions.

Read this section
M2-Q02 rubric - 100 points total
Dimension Excellent Adequate Needs work Points
Documentation All three entries are complete and clearly labelled: tool named, prompt shown, full output pasted, critique attached. Three entries present but uneven - an output trimmed, or labelling that leaves the reader guessing which output came from where. Fewer than three entries, or entries missing the output, or outputs summarized instead of reproduced. 30
Critique quality Every critique names at least three of the five criteria and cites the specific thing in the output that led to the verdict - a quoted line, a missing requirement, a mis-pitched paragraph. Specific, and short is fine. Critiques name three criteria but support is uneven - some point at the evidence, others assert a verdict (“this one was better written”) without saying what showed it. Fewer than three criteria per output, or critiques are general impressions of quality with nothing in the output cited. 30
Cross-tool synthesis Identifies at least one pattern across the three outputs - something they all did, or a dimension on which they diverged - states what it reveals about the prompt or the task, and names one concrete change to the prompt. A comparison is present and describes differences between the outputs, but stops at description - no pattern named, or no link back to the prompt or the task, or no prompt change proposed. Little comparison, or the synthesis is a ranking of the tools. One run each is not evidence about a product, and a verdict of that kind is not credited whichever way it goes. 20
Prompt quality The prompt demonstrates at least three of the five anatomy elements (role, task, context, constraints, format), used deliberately - each one doing visible work for this particular task. Three elements are present but at least one is generic - a constraint that constrains nothing, a format line that restates the task. Fewer than three elements, or the prompt is a single unstructured request. 20

The Studio

Ninety minutes, four blocks. Each block earns one part of the grade.

Opener - the task and the prompt

15 minutes Earns: Prompt quality

Pick the task and write the prompt. No tools open yet.

Before you write anything, finish this sentence about the task you picked: “The better answer will be the one that ______.” If you cannot finish it, pick a different task.

Then write the prompt, using the five anatomy elements by name: role, task, context, constraints, format. You need at least three of them doing real work.

Try this

Write the prompt before you open anything. The temptation is to start typing into a tool and shape the request as you go - which is a fine way to work and the wrong way to run a comparison. Everything you learn here comes from the prompt being the same all three times.

Work block 1 - three runs

30 minutes Earns: Documentation

Run the prompt in all three tools and paste what comes back. About ten minutes each is the pace this block is built for.

Open Gemini (or another tool if you prefer) for the first run, then your two other picks.

Caution

Send the same prompt every time - do not improve it between runs. Refining a prompt across turns is a real skill and you practised it last week; it is a different exercise. Here the prompt is the thing held still, so that what varies is the part worth looking at. Paste each output in full, including the parts you would have cut.

Work block 2 - three critiques

25 minutes Earns: Critique quality

Read the three outputs and write a critique of each. Two to four sentences is plenty.

Note

Point at what produced the judgement. “Missed the 200-word limit by half” is a critique. “Well written” is a rating. The difference is whether someone reading your casebook later can see what you saw.

Closing - synthesis and reflection

20 minutes Earns: Cross-tool synthesis

Two things, in order.

The synthesis. One pattern across the three outputs, what it reveals about your prompt or your task, and one change you would make to the prompt.

0 words

Then your Module 2 reflection. Looking back across this module - generative AI and how it differs from what you met in Module 1, the anatomy of a prompt, refining across a conversation, having a tool draft a prompt for you, and the frontier-versus-local landscape - answer the following in 3–5 sentences:

  1. Which single change to how you write prompts has made the most difference to what you get back?
  2. What do you now notice about an AI-generated answer that you would have read straight past in Week 1?
  3. What is one thing from this module you expect to still be doing in a year?

Before you export

Tick these as you check them. The ticks aren’t saved, sent, or seen by anyone - they are here so you can walk the list once without losing your place.
