Your post

One paragraph or two — specific beats broad.

  • which gap you found, and how you knew (a count, a quoted headline, a source pattern);
  • who it would affect if a real tool were trained on data like this;
  • one thing you'd change about how the data was collected or labeled.

Your two replies

Not “good point” — pick one of these.

  • connect their finding to something in your own analysis (agreement or contradiction — contradictions are more interesting);
  • ask what would have to be true for their mitigation to actually work;
  • offer a gap they didn't look for.
What a weak post and a strong post look like

Weak

“I noticed the dataset had a lot more politics stories than international ones. This seems biased and could cause problems if an AI learned from it. They should collect more balanced data.”

Everything in it is true. None of it is checkable, and nobody can reply to it with anything but agreement. There’s no count, no example, and “more balanced data” doesn’t say balanced along what.

Stronger

“Politics, Business and Technology are 37 of the 50 headlines — Technology alone is 10, and 9 of those come from one outlet, TechPulse. So ‘technology news’ in this dataset largely means ‘what one publication decided was technology news.’ A summarizer trained on this would learn one outlet’s beat as the shape of an entire subject area, and a reader would never see the omission, because missing coverage doesn’t look like anything. The fix isn’t more tech stories — it’s a source cap, so no single outlet can supply most of a category.”

Same observation. The difference is that it counts something, names the outlet, says who it lands on, and proposes a change specific enough to argue with. Notice that the last sentence is the part most worth replying to — someone could reasonably say a source cap would just produce thinner coverage. That’s a discussion.
