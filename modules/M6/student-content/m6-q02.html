What You’ll Do

The revision Module 3 promised — line by line, with a reason for every decision.

Read this section

Revise your Personal AI Code into version 2: go through version 1 line by line, decide what to keep, change, remove or add, and give a reason for every decision. It’s Module 6’s second graded artifact. In Module 3 you wrote the Code you believed then. Since then the course has covered bias, privacy, work, governance and where AI is heading — and you’ve used these tools for three more months.

The four parts

  • Part 1 — version 1, as you wrote it: copied in, so the changes are visible.
  • Part 2 — your guidelines, decided: keep, change or remove each one, add new ones, and say why.
  • Part 3 — purpose, disclosure and review, revised: the same, for the rest of the Code.
  • Part 4 — does it hold together? A check that version 2 is one Code, not a list of patches.

Where’s version 1?

  • In your CSC141-portfolio folder, as 09-ai-code-v1.pdf.
  • Lost it? Open the M3-Q03 assignment in Canvas: your submitted file is still there to download.
  • Never wrote one? Contact your instructor through Canvas Inbox before you start.

Format & length

  • Typed into this page. The export prints version 2 clean, then a table of what changed and why — about two to three pages.
Note

“Keep” is a real decision. A guideline that survived three months of the course is worth keeping — say why it held up. A Code where nothing changed would be a finding too, but you’d have to show your reasoning for every line.

What to Draw On

Every reason should name something specific.

Read this section

A reason like “I learned more about AI” could be anyone’s. A reason like “M4L5: I now take names out before I paste anything about other people” is yours. Some places to look:

Module 4

What your bias screening showed. What your security assessment found about the tools you use, and the steps you chose. The three lenses.

Module 5

Your job as tasks, and which ones need human judgment. The workflow you tested and where it broke. The evidence on deskilling. Your mini-project’s ethics.

Module 6

The rules that do and don’t exist yet. AI that acts on its own, with your accounts. What you’re paying for, and what you’d lose.

Your own experience

A time a tool was wrong and you caught it — or didn’t. A rule from version 1 you actually broke, and why. Something you started doing without writing it down.

Version 2 still needs everything version 1 did: a purpose, at least five guidelines specific enough that someone could tell whether you followed them, covering academic work, personal learning and professional use, a disclosure policy and a review plan.

The Studio

About 75 minutes, four blocks.

Opener — version 1, as you wrote it

15 minutes

Copy your version 1 in: its purpose, disclosure policy and review plan here, and each guideline into the first box of a guideline group below. Copy it as it was, without fixing anything yet. The before is what makes the after visible.

Work block 1 — decide every guideline

30 minutes Earns: Revision quality + Justification

For each version 1 guideline: keep it, change it or remove it, and say why. Then add what’s missing.

Note

The same test as version 1: could someone watching you tell whether you’d followed it? “Be careful with agents” is a value. “No AI tool sends, buys or submits anything in my name without me reading it first” is a guideline.
