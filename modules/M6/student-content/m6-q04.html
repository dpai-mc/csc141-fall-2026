Which Track You’re On

Every student gives one short presentation in this course.

Read this section
  • Portfolio track — Monday, December 7, in our live session. About four to five minutes walking the class through your semester’s work, then one question from the class. Most students are on this track. Everything you need is on this page.
  • Topic track — already done. If you gave an approved topic presentation earlier in the semester, that was your presentation. On December 7, come ready to listen and ask at least one good question.

Either way, you still hand in your full portfolio on Thursday, December 10. The presentation is separate from it.

Note

Can’t be there live on December 7? Contact your instructor through Canvas Inbox before then to arrange an alternative.

The Portfolio Track

Three things, four to five minutes, one question.

Read this section
  1. Your strongest piece of work (about two minutes). Pick one file from your portfolio. Say what it was, what you did, and what it shows about what you can do now. Show one part of it — a finding, a table, a paragraph — on screen, or read it aloud.
  2. Your AI Code’s headline (about one minute). The one guideline that matters most to you — and, if it changed between version 1 and version 2, what changed it.
  3. One lesson learned (about one minute). Something you think differently about now than in week 1. Your 10 Things list is a good place to look.
  4. One question from the class, answered live.

What helps

  • Practicing once out loud, with a timer.
  • Showing the actual work, not describing it in general.
  • One honest limitation or surprise — it’s often the best part.
  • Saying “I don’t know, but here’s how I’d find out” when that’s the true answer.

What doesn’t

  • Slides with walls of text. Slides aren’t required at all.
  • Summarizing every module. Choose; four minutes goes fast.
  • Reading a script word for word.
  • Showing anything with a classmate’s or another private person’s information in it.

Asking questions is part of it too. When classmates present, be ready to ask one real question — about a choice they made, a result that surprised them, or how they’d use it outside the course.
