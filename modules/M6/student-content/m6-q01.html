What You’ll Do

One AI issue, one decision-maker, one specific recommendation.

Read this section

Write a short brief recommending one specific action on one AI issue, addressed to someone who has the power to take it. It’s Module 6’s first graded artifact, and M6L2’s activity. Governance approaches are in M6L1; how decisions get made, and how people influence them, is in M6L2. This page is where you write it.

Choose your starting point

  • Your field — an AI use in the field you studied in Module 5. Your mini-project’s ethical analysis is a natural place to start.
  • Your 10 Things list — an item that raises a governance question.
  • A rule from M6L1 — the law or policy you researched, or another one: should it be adopted, changed or applied somewhere else?

The four parts

  • The problem — a specific AI governance issue, who it affects, and evidence.
  • Your recommendation — one specific action the decision-maker can take.
  • Why — your justification, through an ethical framework from the course.
  • The strongest objection — stated fairly, and answered.

Format & length

  • A memo to a real kind of decision-maker: a city council, a school board, a college, an employer, a professional association, a state legislator. A brief addressed to “society” has no one to act on it.
  • One to two pages — about 400 to 800 words across the four parts. Short is a feature: decision-makers read short.
Note

You can take any side. AI policy questions are often political. Your brief is graded on its reasoning — evidence, a clear proposal, an ethical framework applied honestly, and the other side answered — not on which position you take.

Note

Using AI on this brief. You can use Gemini or another tool to find sources, test your argument or tighten your wording. The recommendation and the reasoning must be yours, every source you cite must be one you’ve opened yourself, and the last block asks how you used AI.

Caution

Address a public body or a kind of organization, not a private person. “The Ukiah City Council” or “a community college’s academic senate” is fine; your manager by name isn’t. Don’t include any private person’s information.

The Studio

About 90 minutes: an opener, four blocks and a closing note.

Opener — your issue and your audience

10 minutes Earns: Problem identification

Choose your starting point, the decision-maker, and a one-line subject.

Work block 1 — the problem

20 minutes Earns: Problem identification

Say what the problem is, who it affects, and what evidence shows it’s real.

At least one piece of evidence should come from a source you’ve opened yourself — name it and give its date. Course pages cite their sources; you can use those, too.

Work block 2 — your recommendation

25 minutes Earns: Policy proposal

Recommend one specific action your audience can actually take.

Note

“AI should be regulated” is a wish. “Before the city buys any system that records license plates, the council should require a public hearing and a written policy on how long the data is kept and who can search it” is a recommendation. The test: could your audience vote on it, or put it in a policy, next month?

Work block 3 — why: your ethical justification

20 minutes Earns: Ethical justification

Justify your recommendation through one framework from the course, applied to this case.

Note

Your choices. One of M4L5’s lenses for judging a use — harms and benefits, rights, fairness — or one of M6L1’s approaches to writing rules — risk-based, principle-based, transparency-based. A lens works well when your recommendation is about one use; an approach works well when it’s about a rule for many.

Work block 4 — the strongest objection

15 minutes Earns: Counterargument

State the best argument against your recommendation the way its supporters would — then answer it.

If you can’t state the other side well enough that someone who holds it would agree with your wording, you haven’t found the strongest objection yet. M6L1’s federal-versus-state section is a model of stating both sides fairly.

Words in the four parts: 0 · aim for about 400–800
