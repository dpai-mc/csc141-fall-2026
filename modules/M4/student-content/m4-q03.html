What You’ll Do

The AI tools in your life, what they do with your information, and what you’ll do about it.

Read this section

Take stock of the AI tools you use, find out what each one does with your information, and decide what you’ll do about it. It’s Module 4’s second graded artifact — and M4L5’s activity. It closes with your Module 4 reflection. Where to find the answers, how to share less and when a local model makes sense are in M4L5; this page is where you do it.

The five parts

  • Part 1 — inventory: every AI tool you use or plan to use, including AI built into other apps.
  • Part 2 — evaluation: for the three you use most, what each collects, where it’s kept and who can see it, whether it trains on what you type, how long it keeps it and what deleting reaches — and where you found each answer.
  • Part 3 — protection steps: one specific step for each of those three tools.
  • Part 4 — the local-model question: for one of your own uses, would a model on your own hardware be worth what it costs?
  • Part 5 — your overall judgment: is the privacy cost of your tools worth it?

Format & length

  • Typed into this page. About two to three pages exported.
  • Module reflection: 3–5 sentences answering all three prompts.
Caution

This assessment goes to your instructor. List tools, not accounts — never write an email address, username or password here. If there’s a tool you’d rather not name, describe the kind instead (“a chat assistant in a social app”). You can earn full credit either way.

Work block 1 — evaluate your three most-used tools

30 minutes Earns: Evaluation

For the three tools you use most, find the answers — and write down where you found each one.

M4L4’s four questions: what is collected, where it’s kept, who can see it, and how long it lasts. For Gemini, Copilot, ChatGPT and Claude, the course page How the tools handle your data has sourced answers. For anything else, check the tool’s settings and search its privacy policy. “Not sure” is an honest answer — say what you’d need to find out.

Work block 2 — one protection step per tool

20 minutes Earns: Protection steps

Choose one specific step for each of your three tools, and make it checkable.

Note

“Be careful what I share” is advice. “Before I paste anything from work into Tool B, I replace every name with a role” is a step. The test: could someone watching you tell whether you’d done it?

Work block 3 — the local-model question

15 minutes Earns: Local-model judgment

Pick one of your own uses and decide whether a model on your own hardware would be worth it.

Use M2L5’s four questions. If you kept your M2L5 comparison, its two columns are a head start. You don’t need to install anything.

Work block 4 — your overall judgment

10 minutes Earns: Overall judgment

Answer this week’s question for your own tools: is the privacy cost worth it — and where’s your line?

Reason through at least one of M4L5’s lenses — harms and benefits, rights or fairness. Bring this to Monday’s session.

Closing — your Module 4 reflection

20 minutes Portfolio

Looking back across this module — where bias comes from, what “fair” can mean, what fixing it can and can’t do, what happens to data, and what you can do about your own — answer the following in 3–5 sentences:

  1. Which finding from your own work in this module — the screening, the re-run or this assessment — surprised you most, and why?
  2. Your re-run tested the one fix you control in a prompt, and this assessment tested the protections you control in your own tools. What did each show you about the limits of what one user can do?
  3. Name one thing you’ll do differently with AI tools after this module, and one problem you think only an organization or a law could fix.
