What You’ll Do

Audit the AI screening you’ve been building for three weeks.

Read this section

Audit one AI output the way a careful reviewer would: what it decided, where bias could have entered, whether it was fair — and by which definition — whether it’s an acceptable use, and what would fix it. It’s the first of Module 4’s two graded artifacts. The output you’re auditing is the one you’ve been building since M4L1: the eight-applicant screening.

Bring your three records

  • m4l1-screening-record.pdf — the tool’s first answer and your first two bias sources.
  • m4l2-fairness-check.pdf — your three fairness judgments.
  • m4l3-rerun-comparison.pdf — the re-run with added instructions, and the counts for both runs.

Missing one? The section below has both prompts. Run what you need — it takes a few minutes — and say in Part 1 that you did.

This assignment’s AI rule. The screening is AI output — that’s what you’re auditing. The audit is yours: the sources, the judgments, the critique and the recommendations are your reasoning, in your words. If an AI tool helps with any part of it, say which part and how in Part 6.

The six parts

  • Part 1 — what was audited: the tool, both answers as it wrote them, every decision, and an accuracy check.
  • Part 2 — three sources of bias, each tested against your two runs.
  • Part 3 — the fairness definitions, applied to both runs.
  • Part 4 — your judgment: is this an acceptable use, through at least one lens?
  • Part 5 — two or more recommendations you could check.
  • Part 6 — any AI use in this audit.

Format & length

  • Typed or pasted into this page. Both answers go in exactly as the tool wrote them.
  • About four to six pages exported
  • About two to four pages exported.
    • mdash; the appendix holds both answers and the prompt.

The eight applicants are fictional, so nothing in this audit is about a real person. Your audit goes to your instructor.

Work block 2 — the fairness definitions, both runs

15 minutes Earns: Fairness definitions

Apply at least two of the three definitions to both runs, using the counts.

The counts are in the opener — press Count both runs if you haven’t. Reminders, from M4L2:

  • Demographic parity — A fairness criterion: each group receives the positive outcome at the same rate, regardless of qualifications.
  • Equalized odds — A fairness criterion: among people who truly qualify, each group receives the positive outcome at the same rate — and among people who don’t, each group is wrongly given it at the same rate.
  • Individual fairness — A fairness criterion: people who are alike in every way that matters to the decision receive the same outcome.

Work block 3 — your judgment

20 minutes Earns: Ethical judgment

Decide whether a tool like this should be used to screen job applicants — and give your reasons through at least one lens.

Note

The three lenses, from M4L5. Harms and benefits — who is helped, who is harmed, and does the good outweigh the harm for everyone affected? Rights — did the people affected agree, and can they find out what happened and challenge it? Fairness — are people alike in the ways that matter treated alike, and do the costs fall on the same group every time?

Work block 4 — recommendations

15 minutes Earns: Recommendations

Recommend at least two specific changes, and say how you’d know each one worked.

Note

“Make the tool fairer” is a wish. “Every time the vendor updates the tool, the employer runs the eight matched pairs through it before using it again, and doesn’t use it if any pair is treated differently” is a recommendation — it says where, who, what, and how you’d check.
