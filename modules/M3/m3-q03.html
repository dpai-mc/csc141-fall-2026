What You’ll Do

Your own rules for when and how you’ll use AI — then your Module 3 reflection.

Read this section

Your Personal AI Code, version 1: your own rules for when and how you’ll use AI. It’s Module 3’s second graded artifact, and it closes with your Module 3 reflection. What a Code is and what makes a guideline good are in this week’s lesson; this page is where you write it.

The four parts

  • Purpose statement — why you use AI, and what you’re protecting.
  • Guidelines — at least five, specific enough that someone could tell whether you’d followed them, covering academic work, personal learning and professional use.
  • Disclosure policy — when you’ll tell people about your AI use, and how.
  • Review plan — when you’ll reread it, and what would make you change it.

Keep it. In Module 6 you’ll revise this into version 2 — you’ll need this exact file then.

Format & length

  • Typed into this page, or pasted in from wherever you drafted it.
  • About one to two pages exported. Module reflection: 3–5 sentences answering all three prompts.

The Studio

About 85 minutes, four blocks.

Opener — purpose statement

10 minutes Earns: Purpose statement

Write two or three sentences on why you use AI and what you want to protect.

Your Code goes to your instructor and into your portfolio. Write what’s true for you — you never have to include anything you’d rather keep private.

Work block 1 — guidelines

35 minutes Earns: Actionable guidelines + Contexts

Write at least five guidelines, and tag each with the situation it’s for.

Note

“Always verify” is a value. “Any fact from an AI tool that goes into work I hand in, I check in a source I’ve opened myself” is a guideline. The test: could someone watching you tell whether you’d followed it?

Closing — your Module 3 reflection

20 minutes Portfolio

Looking back across this module — how an AI answer gets made and how to check one, using AI to learn rather than skip learning, crediting AI honestly, and writing your own rules — answer the following in 3–5 sentences:

  1. Which habit from this module — checking sources, noticing what you offload, or disclosing — do you expect to be hardest to keep, and why?
  2. Describe one moment in this module when an AI answer, or your own use of AI, surprised you.
  3. How has what you mean by “using AI honestly” changed since Week 1?
