What You’ll Do

A study tool for a real class, built from sources you chose, checked, and tested on yourself.

Read this section

Build a study tool for a class you’re taking, from sources you chose — then check it, use it, and find out whether it taught you anything. It’s one of Module 3’s two graded artifacts. Why it’s built this way is in this week’s lesson and in M3L2; this page is where you do it.

This assignment’s AI rule: open, with disclosure. The study aids will be AI-generated — that’s the point. What must be yours: the choice of sources, the checking, and the writing in Parts 5 and 6. Every AI interaction goes in your log.

The seven parts

  • Part 1 — your class, topic and route (a Gemini notebook, a Gemini chat with your files, or a Gemini chat with your notes pasted in — all three count the same).
  • Part 2 — your sources: at least three you opened and are allowed to use, listed in one style guide.
  • Part 3 — your AI log: every interaction — which tool, what you asked or set up, what you used.
  • Part 4 — the study tool itself: what it generated — at least ten quiz questions or flashcards, or a study guide — pasted in.
  • Part 5 — five items checked against your sources.
  • Part 6 — the learning test: use it, close it, and explain the topic in your own words (150–250 words), then say what the tool did and didn’t do for you.
  • Part 7 — your disclosure statement.

Format & length

  • Typed or pasted into this page. Part 4 goes in as generated — don’t tidy it.
  • Sources: three or more. Checks: five. Learning test: 150–250 words. Style guide: one, throughout.

APA A reference-list entry, plus an in-text citation — and a sentence in your text saying how you used the tool. Company. (Year). Tool name [Description]. URL Google. (2026). Gemini [Large language model]. https://gemini.google.com — in text: (Google, 2026)
MLA A Works Cited entry for content you quote or paraphrase; other uses (editing, translating) acknowledged in a note or in your text. “Description of your prompt” prompt. Chicago A footnote or endnote. Whether it also needs a bibliography entry varies — check with your instructor. Tool name, response to “your prompt,” Company, date. 1. Gemini, response to “Explain how a bill becomes law in California,” Google, September 28, 2026.

How It’s Graded

50 points, four dimensions.

Read this section
M3-Q02 rubric — 50 points total
DimensionExcellentAdequateNeeds workPoints
Disclosure Every AI interaction is in the log — tool, what was asked or set up, what was used — and the disclosure statement matches the log and says which parts are AI-generated. The log is there but thin, or doesn’t match the statement. No log, or a disclosure statement that doesn’t say what the AI did. 15
Verification Five items from the study tool, each checked against a named place in your sources, with what the source actually says and a clear verdict. Any item that isn’t supported is named as such. Five items, but at least one check is vague about where in the source it looked, or only confirms the topic rather than the answer. Fewer than five, or checks that consist of asking the tool whether it was right. 15
Learning test 150–250 words explaining the topic without the tool, in your own words, plus an honest account of what the tool helped with and what it didn’t. The explanation is there but thin, or the account of what the tool did is missing or generic. No explanation written without the tool, or one that restates the tool’s output. 10
Sources & citation Three or more sources you’re allowed to use, listed consistently in one recognized style; the study tool itself credited as AI-generated. Three sources, with a mixed format or a missing detail. Fewer than three, no consistent style, or sources you weren’t permitted to upload. 10

Scored by the instructor and entered in the Canvas gradebook, in the Module artifacts category.

The Studio

About 105 minutes, five blocks. Each work block earns one part of the grade.

Opener — class, topic, route, sources

10 minutes

Pick a class and a topic you’ll be tested on or have to write about, choose your route, and gather at least three sources. Your own notes are always fine; so are readings you were given to study. Instructor slides only if your instructor says you may upload them. Never a classmate’s work or anyone’s personal information. Your topic, sources and study tool all go to your instructor.

Note

Built a notebook or chat in M3L2’s activity? Build on it here — add a source and keep going.

  • Route A — a Gemini notebook. Create a notebook and add your sources to it.
  • Route B — a Gemini chat with your files. Upload your sources to a new chat.
  • Route C — a Gemini chat with your notes pasted in. Paste the text of your sources into a new chat. Nothing to upload.

All three routes earn full credit. Some notebook features are limited to students 18 and over, even on a college account; routes B and C need nothing more than the Gemini you already use.

Work block 1 — build it, and log as you go

30 minutes Earns: Disclosure

Load your sources, have the tool make a study aid, and log every interaction the moment it happens. Open Gemini (or another tool if you prefer) by your route. Ask for at least ten quiz questions or flashcards, or a study guide, using only your sources. Paste what it made into Part 4 as it came back.

Using only the sources I've added, make [ten quiz questions / ten flashcards / a one-page study guide] on [topic]. For each item, note which source it comes from.
Caution

Log it now, not later. Five minutes after a conversation you’ll remember the answer and forget what you asked — and the log is what makes your disclosure true.

Work block 2 — check five items

25 minutes Earns: Verification

Pick five items from your study tool and check each against your sources. Find the place that supports the answer — the M3L1 method. “Built from your sources” doesn’t mean right: a tool can misread a page or attach an answer to the wrong one. An item that isn’t supported is a finding, not a failure.

Work block 3 — use it, then the learning test

25 minutes Earns: Learning test

Study with your tool for fifteen minutes. Then close it, and write about the topic without looking back.

Note

This is the part the grade is really about. A study tool that feels useful and a study tool that taught you something are different things, and only this block tells them apart.
