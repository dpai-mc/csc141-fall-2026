What This Tool Is

Gemini is Google's conversational AI assistant, and it's the baseline tool for CSC 141 — the one every student already has, free, through their college Google Workspace account. Demonstrations use it, walkthroughs match it, and any activity that doesn't say otherwise assumes it.

Baseline means floor, not ceiling. Gemini isn't here because it's "the best" — it's here because everyone has it, which is what lets a step-by-step instruction mean the same thing to the whole class. Once you're comfortable, use whatever suits the task. Forming your own view about which tool fits which job is a good chunk of what this course is for.

Note

The same account also includes NotebookLM, a separate Google tool for working with documents you upload. It gets its own guide later in the course.

Getting Access

Five minutes. This is the one thing you need working before anything else in the course.

  1. Sign in to your college email. Go to the college Gmail login and sign in with your @student.mendocino.edu address. (Note the student. part — @mendocino.edu without it is the staff and faculty domain.) If you're already signed in on this device, skip to step 3.
  2. Open Gemini. Go directly to gemini.google.com in a new tab, or use the Gemini entry point inside Gmail. Because you're signed into your college Google account, it should load without a separate signup.
  3. Confirm you're on the right account. Check the account avatar in the top-right corner and make sure it shows your @student.mendocino.edu address, not a personal Gmail.
  4. Test it. Type Say hello and press Enter. If you get a response, you're set.
Caution

Step 3 is the one people get wrong. Landing in Gemini on a personal Google account still works, so nothing obviously breaks — but you'll hit the personal free-tier limits instead of the college ones, and your coursework ends up mixed into a personal account. If Gemini ever asks you to pay, check the avatar first. That's almost always the reason.

It still won't work — now what?
  • Re-check step 3. Wrong-account is the most common cause by a wide margin.
  • Try gemini.google.com directly rather than the Gmail entry point.
  • Still stuck? Contact the college IT help desk and tell them you need Gemini access for CSC 141.
  • While you're waiting, use Microsoft Copilot instead — copilot.microsoft.com, signing in with the same @student.mendocino.edu address. It's also college-provided (web only), and every activity in this course can be completed in it. Don't let an access problem stall your week-1 work.

The Interface

What you're looking at when it loads.

Gemini opens on a mostly-empty screen with a text box. That's deliberate — almost everything happens by typing into that box.

Core Workflow

Three short exercises. About 12 minutes total, and you'll have used everything this course needs.

1. Ask, then follow up

5 minutes

Your task: see that a conversation carries context from one message to the next.

  1. Type this and send it: 
    Explain what a community college is to someone who has never heard of one.
  2. When it answers, send just this — nothing else: 
    Now say that again in half as many words.

Notice that you never repeated the topic. It knew what "that" referred to, because everything in one conversation stays in view.

2. Attach something

5 minutes

Your task: ask a question about a file rather than about the world.

  1. Find any document or image on your computer — a syllabus, a photo, an assignment sheet. Nothing personal or sensitive.
  2. Use the attach control next to the prompt box to upload it.
  3. Ask something specific about it, for example: "What are the three most important dates in this document?"
Caution

Never upload anything containing your student ID, your grades, another student's work, or anyone's personal information. This holds for every AI tool, not just this one.

3. Start clean

2 minutes

Your task: learn when to abandon a conversation.

Click New chat and ask your follow-up question from exercise 1 again — "Now say that again in half as many words." It won't know what you mean, because a new chat starts with nothing.

That's the useful lesson: when a long conversation starts drifting or giving worse answers, start a new one. Long threads accumulate everything you've said, including your wrong turns, and the tool keeps trying to stay consistent with all of it.

Free-Tier Limits

What your college account gets you.

Your college Workspace account gives you Gemini at no cost. What that includes — which model version, how many messages, how large an upload — depends on how the college has configured the account, and Google changes these terms regularly.

Verify

Check this one yourself rather than trusting a number printed here. Any specific limit written into a course guide is wrong within a term or two. Open Gemini's own help pages while signed in on your college account — that view reflects your actual entitlements, which a guide can't. If you hit a wall mid-assignment, that's worth telling your instructor about, because it affects everyone in the class.

Practically, for this course: nothing we ask you to do comes close to a usage ceiling. The activities are short, and none of them require uploading large files.

Privacy & Data Handling

Worth reading properly. This is course content, not fine print.

Students on a college account often assume "the school gave it to me, so it's private." Those are two different things, and the gap between them is genuinely worth understanding — not just here, but for every institutional tool you'll ever use.

Three separate questions, which have three different answers:

  1. Is your conversation used to train Google's models? Consumer accounts and education Workspace accounts are treated differently, and the education terms are generally more restrictive.
  2. Can the college see your conversations? A Workspace administrator's visibility into an education account is not the same as a private account's, and it's a fair thing to ask about.
  3. How long is anything kept? Gemini has activity and history controls; where they live and what they default to changes over time.
Verify

Look these up rather than taking anyone's word — including this guide's. Sign in on your college account, open Gemini's privacy and activity settings, and read what it actually says about your account. If you want the institutional answer to question 2, ask the college IT help desk directly; that's a reasonable question and they can answer it authoritatively. Practising this — going to the source instead of trusting a summary — is a skill this whole course is built around.

Caution

Regardless of what the settings say: never enter your student ID, your grades, other students' work, or anyone's personal information into Gemini or any other AI tool. This rule doesn't depend on the privacy policy, and it doesn't change when the policy does.

Known Weaknesses

The failure modes you'll actually run into this semester.

Every section above tells you what this tool does. This one tells you where it falls over — which matters more, because these are the failures that cost people marks and credibility.

  • It is confidently wrong. Incorrect answers arrive in exactly the same assured tone as correct ones. There is no wobble in its voice when it doesn't know something, so you cannot use confidence as a signal.
  • It invents sources. Ask for citations and you may get references formatted perfectly — author, title, year, journal — for work that does not exist. Module 3 looks at this closely, because it's the failure most likely to damage your academic work.
  • It isn't consistent. The same prompt twice can produce meaningfully different answers. If you're relying on something it told you, check it; don't assume you'd get the same reply again.
  • Its knowledge has an edge. There's a point past which it wasn't trained on anything. It may or may not look something up for you, and it doesn't always make clear which it did.
  • It agrees with you too readily. Push back on a correct answer and it will often fold and "correct" itself into being wrong. Disagreement from you isn't evidence, and it shouldn't be treated as such — but it frequently is.
Try this

Ask Gemini a factual question you already know the answer to, then tell it — confidently and incorrectly — that it's wrong. Watch what it does. It's a two-minute exercise and it will change how you read every answer it gives you afterwards.
