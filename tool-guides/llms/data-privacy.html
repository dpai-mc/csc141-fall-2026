Accurate as of August 2026. AI tools change faster than a semester does. If what you see on screen doesn’t match this guide, trust the screen — and tell your instructor so it can be fixed. Noticing that gap is itself an AI-literacy skill.

The One-Sentence Version

The protections you get depend on the account you sign in with, not on which AI tool you picked. The same company can treat the same conversation two completely different ways depending on whether you signed in with @student.mendocino.edu or with a personal address you made up.

The college has agreements with Google and Microsoft that a personal account simply isn’t covered by. This page shows what each tool does with your data. Every claim here is sourced and dated, because all of it changes.

What the Table Doesn’t Say

These are the distinctions students collapse, and getting them apart is most of the value of the page.

“Not used for training” is not “nobody can see it” — and there are two different admins

This is the distinction the whole page turns on. Google and Microsoft commit that their own staff don’t review this data and that it doesn’t train their models23. For Google, that commitment is the documented Higher Education default — the college’s own tenant configuration is still unconfirmed (see the Gemini row in the table). Your college’s administrators are a separate party with separate powers, and on both college tools those powers are real and documented: Microsoft logs prompts and responses in the college’s own M365 tenant, reachable via administrator audit and eDiscovery; Google Vault lets a Workspace administrator search Gemini prompts and responses by user and date and export them. All of it is true at once. The vendor stepping back is not the institution stepping back, and students hear “protected” and assume both.

Note

This is not a scandal — it’s how institutional accounts work everywhere, and it’s usually why the protections exist at all. The accurate version is narrower and more useful: an identifiable party with a documented process can retrieve it if there’s a reason to.

“The college gave it to me” is not “it’s private”

An institutional account means an institution is party to it. That’s often better than a personal account — there’s an agreement, and someone accountable — but it is a different thing from privacy, and the difference is worth being deliberate about.

A setting is not a property

Whether a tool trains on your inputs is, in several of these products, a toggle — and its default varies by vendor and changes over time. It isn’t a fact about “AI.” It’s a fact about one company’s current configuration. Module 1, Lesson 5 (M1L5) goes into why that distinction matters.

Caution

The rule that doesn’t depend on any of the above: never enter your student ID, your grades, other students’ work, or anyone’s personal information into any AI tool. This rule doesn’t change when a policy changes, and it applies identically on a college account and a personal one.

Sources and Dates

Every source behind the table, each with the date it was checked. All entries verified 2026-08-19.

Verify

Every claim on this page has a source and a date next to it, and you should click them. A page about who to trust with your data that asked you to trust it without evidence would be a poor advertisement for the course.

  1. Google (administrator access) — checked 2026-08-19. Google Vault covers Gemini app conversations for Education customers as of 2025-05-09: admins can “search prompts and responses with a number of filters, such as user and date” and “export conversations in an XML format… via the UI or API.” Aggregate usage is visible under Generative AI → Gemini Reports. Note the boundary: Vault covers the Gemini app, not in-app Workspace features like Help me write, for which “no prompts or responses are retained.” (Source: Google Workspace Updates.)

  2. Google (training) — checked 2026-08-19. Gemini app and NotebookLM became Core Services for Google Workspace for Education on 2025-04-03, bringing them under the Workspace for Education terms with enterprise-grade data protection: “their data is not human reviewed or used to train AI models.” Enabled ON by default for Higher Education customers (for Mendocino specifically, the college’s own tenant configuration is unconfirmed — see the Gemini row in Section 2). Gemini app is available for ages 13+; NotebookLM requires 18+. (Sources: Google Workspace Updates blog; Google Workspace Admin Help — Gemini for Google Workspace FAQ — Education.)

  3. Microsoft — checked 2026-08-19. “Prompts and responses aren’t used to train the underlying foundation models” on work/school accounts; enterprise data protection is indicated by a green shield next to New Chat; prompts and responses are logged and stored in Exchange within the tenant under its retention policies, with administrator audit logging and eDiscovery. (Source: Microsoft Learn — Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat Privacy and Protections.)

  4. OpenAI (training) — checked 2026-08-19. “When you use our services for individuals such as ChatGPT and Codex, we may use your content to train our models”; opt out via Data Controls or the privacy portal’s “do not train on my content”; Temporary Chat “won’t appear in history, use or create memories, or be used to train our models.” (Source: OpenAI Help Center — How your data is used to improve model performance.)

  5. OpenAI (settings and retention) — checked 2026-08-19. The training toggle is Settings → Data Controls → “Improve the model for everyone” (it syncs across devices on a signed-in account). Temporary Chats “are deleted from our systems after 30 days.” OpenAI does not publish a retention period for ordinary deleted conversations. (Source: OpenAI Help Center — Data Controls FAQ.)

  6. Anthropic — checked 2026-08-19. Training is opt-in via an in-app prompt, set in Privacy Settings; 30-day retention if declined, five years for new or resumed chats if allowed; rollout from 2025-08-28, existing-user deadline 2025-10-08. (Source: Anthropic — Updates to Consumer Terms and Privacy Policy.)
